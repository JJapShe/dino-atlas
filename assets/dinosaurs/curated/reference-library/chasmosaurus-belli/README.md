# Chasmosaurus belli reference notes

This folder supports anatomy-led review for the six-image `Chasmosaurus belli`
gallery rebuild. Published fossils and figures are evidence controls, not style
targets.

## Evidence boundary used in the gallery

- Direct adult frill evidence: the frill broadens posteriorly into a
  rectangular to triangular shield, reaches more than twice the skull width at
  the orbits, contains paired large parietal fenestrae, and ends in a straight
  or only shallowly embayed posterior bar in `C. belli`.
- Horn gate: brow-horn size varies substantially and is not used alone as a
  species diagnosis. The gallery adopts a modest-horned adult form without
  implying that every adult had identical horn length.
- Direct postcranial and skin evidence: adult CMNFV 2245 is approximately
  4.7 m long, and adult plus juvenile specimens preserve polygonal or
  subrounded body scales with larger rounded feature scales.
- Frill-cover boundary: continuous living skin over the bony fenestrae is a
  conservative life reconstruction. No consulted specimen directly preserves
  the complete frill integument or its colors, so the muted oval fields are
  visual guides to underlying bone geometry rather than fossilized markings.
- Juvenile boundary: UALVP 52613 is an articulated approximately 1.5 m
  juvenile with a short high skull and a narrower frill lacking the adult
  posterior embayment. Its forelimbs are missing, so the juvenile hands and
  complete walking pose remain comparative reconstructions.
- Habitat reconstruction: the lower and middle Dinosaur Park Formation scenes
  use meandering channels, point bars, overbank silt, driftwood, horsetails,
  ferns, low vegetation, and open conifer woodland. Exact water level, season,
  weather, and plant placement remain hypotheses.
- Behavior and association: the distant crested hadrosaur and adult-juvenile
  frames show regional context or a visual growth comparison only. They do not
  establish a species-level identity for the distant animal, predation, herd
  behavior, parentage, sex, kinship, care, hierarchy, or coordinated movement.

## Stable phenotype

- Canonical: celadon-gray to weathered willow body, charcoal-olive saddle,
  pale stone underside, muted mulberry cheek, brick-rose and blue-green frill
  fields, weathered ochre rim, and matte ivory horns and beak.
- Color-pattern variant: smoky violet and deep blue-gray dorsum, broad
  reed-green flank fields, restrained coral shoulder, saffron-clay frill,
  dusty turquoise accents, deep-plum cheek, and a pale neutral underside.
- Reject target rings around the fenestra fields, regular stripes, dots,
  checks, neon accents, a uniform global color cast, or the same broadside
  daylight staging in every role.

## Six gallery roles

1. Neutral whole-body representative with the broad posterior frill and
   shallow posterior embayment.
2. Reverse-facing blue-hour color and pattern variant.
3. Wide Dinosaur Park meander-belt and point-bar habitat.
4. High three-quarter frill anatomy view with continuous skin.
5. Channel-separated regional context with one distant generic crested
   hadrosaurid.
6. Spatially separated adult and juvenile as a cautious visual growth
   comparison.

## Rejection gates

- Reject a tall narrow slab or sail-like frill, deep V-shaped posterior notch,
  open-air holes, transparent membranes, or a frill detached from the skull.
- Reject a heavy rhinoceros body, mammal hooves, one fused forefoot mass,
  extra or merged limbs, fewer than four independently readable feet in a
  complete foreground adult, more than one pelvis-origin tail, or a cropped
  tail tip.
- Reject a large nasal horn, a `Triceratops`-like pair of dominant brow horns,
  a `Styracosaurus` spike fan, a `Pachyrhinosaurus` nasal boss, or identical
  cloned animals in crowded herd scenes.
- The former three v2 project images are superseded for narrow vertical
  frills, rhinoceros-heavy bodies, repeated broadside staging, weak foot
  readability, and crowded cloned-animal ecology.
