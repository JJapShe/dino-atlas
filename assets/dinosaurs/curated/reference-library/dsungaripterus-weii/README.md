# Dsungaripterus weii reference record

이 폴더는 내부 해부 검수용입니다. 도판의 구도나 화풍을 생성 이미지에 복제하지 않고, 화석에서 확인되는 형태 특징만 구조화해 사용합니다.

## Cranial source

- Chen, H. et al. (2020). *New anatomical information on Dsungaripterus weii Young, 1964 with focus on the palatal region*. PeerJ 8:e8741.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.8741
- PMC: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7127482/
- License: CC BY 4.0. Copyright 2020 Chen et al.
- Downloaded: 2026-07-30 through the Europe PMC full-text endpoint and NCBI PMC image endpoints.
- Local files: `zhou-et-al-2020-fulltext.xml`, `pmc-oa-package.xml`, and `peerj-08-8741-g001.jpg` through `peerj-08-8741-g007.jpg`.
- Original figure credits remain in the article captions, including Wei Gao for specimen photography and Xiaocong Guo for drawings where stated.

Identity cues used by Dino Atlas:

- The upper and lower rostral tips are toothless and gently upturned.
- Symmetrical lateral grooves mark the likely edge of a keratinous sheath over the toothless rostrum.
- Teeth are restricted behind the toothless segment. The paper reports 14-15 teeth per side, with low bulbous crowns and broad oval bases.
- Tooth size increases posteriorly to about the fifth position and then decreases.
- Tooth wear facets support hard-object feeding, but do not directly prove a particular prey item or feeding motion.
- A developed sagittal crest begins before the nasoantorbital opening and extends over the posterior skull. Soft-tissue height remains a reconstruction.

Most useful local figures:

- Figure 2 (`peerj-08-8741-g002.jpg`): tooth positions and the long toothless anterior segment in ventral view.
- Figure 3 (`peerj-08-8741-g003.jpg`): toothless upper and lower rostra, sheath-edge grooves, and reconstruction outlines.
- Figure 4 (`peerj-08-8741-g004.jpg`): blunt tooth crowns, wear facets, grooves, and sagittal crest texture.

## Postcranial source

- Song, J., Jiang, S. & Wang, X. (2024). *Postcranial anatomy of Dsungaripterus weii (Pterosauria: Ornithocheiroidea) from the Lower Cretaceous of Wuerho, China*. Journal of Vertebrate Paleontology 44:e2402042.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1080/02724634.2024.2402042
- Only the publisher abstract and data statement were used. No local publisher PDF is stored.
- Relevant conservative cues: comparatively thick limb-bone walls, terrestrial metacarpophalangeal reconstruction, and postcranial differences from *Noripterus*. Generated soft tissue is not treated as direct evidence.
- Morphosource project cited by the paper: https://www.morphosource.org/projects/000657504

## Habitat source

- Xing, L. et al. (2021). *Lower Cretaceous avian-dominated, theropod, thyreophoran, pterosaur and turtle track assemblages from the Tugulu Group, Xinjiang, China: ichnotaxonomy and palaeoecology*. PeerJ 9:e11476.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.11476
- The Wuerho Tugulu Group is described as an inland lacustrine clastic and shallow delta system with distributary channels, interdistributary fine sediment, gray-green sandstone and mudstone interbedded with red-brown mudstone, and a progressively drier depositional signal.
