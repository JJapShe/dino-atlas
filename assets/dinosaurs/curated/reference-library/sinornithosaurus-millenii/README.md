## Evidence boundary used in the gallery

- Direct evidence: a low narrow skull, large orbit, small antorbital opening,
  long forelimbs, three-fingered hands with elongated digits, an enlarged but
  not movie-scale pedal digit-II ungual, a stiffened long tail, and a
  filamentous body covering.
- Reconstruction: exact full feather architecture, compact forearm fringes,
  all colors and markings, stance, weather, habitat moment, and behavior.
- The head close-up deliberately makes no hand, foot, or tail count claim.
- The distant small feathered theropod is not assigned to a species.
- The two separated conspecifics do not establish a pair, family, sex,
  kinship, herd, or coordinated behavior.

## Stable phenotype

- Canonical: charcoal-brown and deep chestnut dorsum, restrained copper cheek
  and shoulder, muted lichen flank, pale flax underside, and irregular broad
  color fields.
- Variant: plum-slate dorsum, lichen-green flanks, copper neck and shoulder,
  and a pale flax underside in restrained blue-hour light.
- Reject regular tail rings, target spots, ladder stripes, a uniform global
  cast, or the same daylight broadside composition in every role.

## Six gallery roles

1. Low horizontal whole-body representative with long arms and filamentous
   covering.
2. Reverse-facing plum, lichen, copper, and flax color-pattern variant.
3. Wide Sihetun tuff-bank, runoff-channel, and lake-margin habitat.
4. Low-skull and neck-filament identity close-up.
5. Channel-separated regional context with one distant generic small
   feathered theropod.
6. Spatially separated conspecific adults as a cautious variation comparison.

## Rejection gates

- Reject a modern bird beak, owl face, enlarged venom fang, giant flight
  wings, `Microraptor`-like hindwing feathers, or a terminal tail fan.
- Reject more or fewer than three fingers on a readable hand, a detached or
  movie-scale sickle claw, hidden or merged feet, extra or fused limbs, more
  than one pelvis-origin tail, or a cropped or forked tail.
- Reject a generic upright ostrich body, a heavy monitor-lizard body, regular
  tail bands, active-eruption spectacle, or captions that assert species-level
  identity or social and predatory relationships not supported by the image.
