## Evidence boundary

- Directly preserved in the historical `Nomingia` holotype MPC-D 100/119: an incomplete postcranial skeleton, complete pelvis, femur, both tibiae and fibulae, 24 caudal vertebrae, and the final five caudals fused into a dagger-like pygostyle.
- The tibia is about 24 percent longer than the femur. The tail is relatively short for a theropod but is not a sudden stump.
- The skull, forelimbs, complete feet, plumage, colors, and terminal feather fan are not directly preserved in the holotype.
- A small terminal fan is a restrained soft-tissue inference from the pygostyle, not a direct fossil observation.
- Funston et al. 2021 regarded `Nomingia gobiensis` as the best-supported junior subjective synonym of `Elmisaurus rarus`, while noting that discovery of diagnostic overlapping material could confirm or refute that interpretation.

## Generation use

- Anchor external proportions to the published tail, pelvis, and hindlimb evidence.
- Keep the skull, three-fingered hands, feet, plumage, and beak explicitly comparative with caenagnathids and `Elmisaurus`.
- Reject modern bird or ostrich heads, giant circular or peacock-like fans, long whip tails, tail stumps, extra limbs, and any image that presents the synonymy as definitively settled.
