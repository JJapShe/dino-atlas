## Evidence boundary

- `Elmisaurus rarus` is a small, gracile caenagnathid from the Nemegt
  Formation. The direct record is incomplete and is strongest for the hands,
  legs, and feet.
- The referred frontal indicates a tall nasal crest that extended onto the
  frontal, possibly comparable in general construction to `Anzu`. The exact
  outline of the crest and the rest of the skull remain incompletely known.
- The hand is slender and has three digits. Digit I is shortest, digit II is
  longest and strongest, and digit III is distinctly thinner. The manual
  unguals are strongly curved but are not giant predatory hooks.
- The tibia is elongate and gracile. The tarsometatarsus is unusually long,
  about 70 percent of femur length in one specimen, and the proximal
  tarsometatarsus becomes consolidated by fusion in mature individuals.
- The foot has three weight-bearing forward toes with digit III longest. A
  greatly reduced digit I is present but does not reach the ground.
- The 2021 synonymy hypothesis would make the long tail and terminal feather
  fan of `Nomingia` applicable to `Elmisaurus`. Keep any fan restrained and
  label it as a comparison-dependent reconstruction.
- Body feathers, arm feathers, skin, color, pattern, and living behavior are
  comparative oviraptorosaur reconstructions rather than direct soft-tissue
  evidence.

## Hard identity gates

- One short, deep caenagnathid-like head with a toothless beak and one tall,
  fore-to-aft nasal crest integrated into the skull roof. Reject a fleshy
  chicken comb, parrot bill, cassowary casque, smooth ostrich head, or lizard
  head.
- The crest must rise from the nasal and extend toward the frontal. Reject the
  old low ridge, a thin vertical fin, paired horns, or a rounded modern-bird
  crown.
- Exactly two forelimbs. Each readable hand must have exactly three total
  digits: short digit I, longest digit II, and a noticeably slender digit III.
- Exactly two hind limbs and two separate feet in complete-body views.
- Each readable foot has three forward weight-bearing toes. No
  dromaeosaur-like enlarged sickle claw and no modern bird perching foot.
- Long, slim lower legs with a compact consolidated ankle-to-metatarsal
  silhouette. Reject ostrich stilts disconnected from a theropod body.
- One continuous balancing tail originating at the pelvis. A small terminal
  fan is allowed only as a restrained synonymy-dependent reconstruction.
- Reject extra heads, arms, hands, legs, feet, fingers, tails, duplicated
  joints, hidden limb counts, or anatomically merged animals.

## Habitat and behavior gates

- Use the Nemegt Formation as a seasonal fluvial floodplain with meandering
  channels, sandy point bars, silty overbank ground, scattered conifers,
  low ferns, horsetails, and sparse flowering vegetation.
- Show seasonal variation through damp channel margins, exposed sand bars,
  shallow water, or dry overbank patches without turning the setting into a
  permanent swamp or an open dune desert.
- Avoid modern grass lawns, flowering meadows, palms, cacti, modern shrubs,
  infrastructure, and a uniform orange color cast.
- Distant regional dinosaurs are context only. Do not imply predation,
  courtship, pair bonding, sex, parenting, or a specific diet without direct
  evidence.

## Consistent phenotype

- Dorsal plumage: cool dove gray and smoky taupe.
- Underside: pale flax to ivory.
- Nasal crest: deep muted wine red with a dark charcoal rear edge.
- Shoulder zone: restrained lichen olive.
- Forelimb feathers: blue-black with a soft steel sheen.
- Beak: matte warm horn gray; lower legs and feet: warm umber-gray scales.
- Pattern: broad irregular shoulder and flank color fields with fine natural
  mottling. No regular dots, zebra bars, rings, checkerboard, or neon colors.
- Tail: body-colored contour feathers with only a modest darker terminal
  feather tuft or fan.

## Six gallery roles

1. Neutral complete-body representative showing the tall integrated nasal
   crest, three-digit hands, two feet, long lower legs, and one continuous
   tail.
2. Reverse-facing dawn walk preserving the same phenotype and anatomy.
3. Wide seasonal Nemegt channel-and-point-bar habitat reconstruction.
4. Intentional head-to-hand anatomy crop showing the integrated crest,
   toothless beak, and exactly three readable fingers.
5. One complete `Elmisaurus` with one distant, fully separated unidentified
   hadrosaurid as cautious regional community context.
6. Two fully separated individuals on an overbank flat as cautious
   size-and-proximity context, with no social or reproductive claim.
