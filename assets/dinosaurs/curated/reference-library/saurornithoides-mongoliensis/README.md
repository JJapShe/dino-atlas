# Saurornithoides mongoliensis reference library

Retrieved: 2026-07-31

This folder separates direct fossil evidence, licensed reference figures, and
generated reconstruction hypotheses. Reference images guide anatomy only; the
generated gallery art does not copy a published life restoration.

## Direct evidence

### Norell et al. 2009

- Citation: Norell, M. A., Makovicky, P. J., Bever, G. S., Balanoff, A. M.,
  Clark, J. M., Barsbold, R. and Rowe, T. 2009. A review of the Mongolian
  Cretaceous dinosaur Saurornithoides (Troodontidae: Theropoda). American
  Museum Novitates 3654, 1-63.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1206/648.1
- BHL record: https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/bibliography/169010
- BHL scan: https://www.biodiversitylibrary.org/itempdf/280747
- Internet Archive mirror:
  https://archive.org/details/reviewmongolian00nore
- License for the BHL digitization: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0.
- Rights holder: American Museum of Natural History Library.
- Local licensed figure pages:
  - `norell-et-al-2009-figure3-holotype-skull-cc-by-nc-sa-4.png`
  - `norell-et-al-2009-figure20-left-pes-cc-by-nc-sa-4.png`
- Evidence used: AMNH FR 6516 is the only definitive specimen. It includes a
  nearly complete but weathered skull and mandibles, four dorsal vertebrae,
  six sacrals, the first four caudals, partial ilium, parts of both pubes and
  ischia, the proximal left femur, an impression of the left tibia, and the
  distal left pes.
- Skull gate: rounded anterior rostrum, elongate narrow jaws, a round orbit
  with a straight jugal beneath it, and numerous small teeth. The paper
  reports 108 teeth for the taxon-level comparison with Zanabazar.
- Foot gate: metatarsal I and digit I are present, digit II preserves its
  complete phalangeal series and a moderately large curved ungual, and digits
  III-IV form the remaining long forward toes. A readable living
  reconstruction therefore uses exactly four digits I-IV, not three forward
  toes plus a raised digit II and hallux.
- Boundary: no forelimb or integument is preserved in AMNH FR 6516. Hand
  structure, filament covering, feather extent, colors, and patterns in the
  generated images are comparative troodontid reconstruction hypotheses.
- Correction: three caudal vertebrae once associated with the holotype are
  reidentified in this paper as protoceratopsian material and are not used to
  reconstruct the Saurornithoides tail.

### Osborn 1924

- Citation: Osborn, H. F. 1924. Three new Theropoda, Protoceratops zone,
  central Mongolia. American Museum Novitates 144, 1-12.
- AMNH record:
  https://digitallibrary.amnh.org/items/60029a9a-5b3a-4aec-a793-9025997185af
- Copyright note: published in 1924; used here as an historical primary
  description and link-only source.
- Evidence used: the type came from the red Djadokhta sandstone at Shabarakh
  Usu/Bayan Zag and has a long slender toothed rostrum.

## Habitat evidence

### Lefeld 1971

- Citation: Lefeld, J. 1971. Geology of the Djadokhta Formation at Bayn Dzak
  (Mongolia). Palaeontologia Polonica 25, 101-127.
- PDF:
  https://www.palaeontologia.pan.pl/Archive/1971_25_101_127_19_21.pdf
- Evidence used: sandy horizons include aeolian deposits, while upper sandy
  and calcareous beds include lacustrine deposition. The generated habitat is
  therefore an aeolian dune and interdune/lake-margin reconstruction rather
  than a dark humid floodplain.
- Boundary: exact shoreline position, weather, water depth, plant density, and
  moment are reconstruction choices.

## Explicit rejects

The three former project files remain archived but are excluded from current
review and gallery selection:

- `saurornithoides-mongoliensis-large-eye-troodontid-imagegen-v1.png`:
  broad modern-bird-like arm fans, unclear hand and foot topology, and a
  generic short-headed troodontid identity.
- `saurornithoides-mongoliensis-violet-turquoise-pattern-imagegen-v1.png`:
  near-duplicate anatomy, the same unstable appendages, and regular tail
  markings that weaken phenotype continuity.
- `saurornithoides-mongoliensis-djadokhta-ecology-imagegen-v1.png`:
  cloned foreground animals, repeated background fauna, broad wing-like arms,
  and under-audited feet in a generic modern-desert scene.

Three early drafts from this rebuild also failed before project copy because a
readable hallux was missing or the supporting foot remained ambiguous. They
remain outside `assets/dinosaurs/`.

## Reconstruction boundaries

- Body filaments, forelimb form, colors, patterns, eye color, and skin are
  comparative hypotheses, not direct soft-tissue evidence for this species.
- The representative palette and the juniper-indigo variant are plausible
  visual systems, not fossil color claims.
- The four-digit foot close-up is a cautious living visualization of the
  preserved I-IV topology, not a fossil footprint or soft-tissue impression.
- Protoceratops and Saurornithoides occur in the Djadokhta context, but the
  gully scene does not claim a documented encounter, predation, or social
  behavior.
- Only the first gallery image is a `count-level pass`. The color role remains
  `review hold`; habitat, skull, foot, and coexistence roles remain
  review/reference images and do not outrank the representative.
