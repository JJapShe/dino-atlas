# Khaan mckennai reference library

This folder is for anatomy review and generation QA. Use the sources to verify
specimen-backed structures and uncertainty; do not copy an existing artwork's
composition, surface treatment, or style.

## Diagnostic sources kept as links

- Clark, Norell, and Barsbold, 2001, original description:
  https://doi.org/10.1671/0272-4634(2001)021%5B0209:TNOTOU%5D2.0.CO;2
  - The paper names `Khaan mckennai` from Ukhaa Tolgod and establishes the
    holotype and referred-specimen context.
- Balanoff and Norell, 2012, detailed osteology:
  https://doi.org/10.1206/803.1
  - Direct BioOne PDF:
    https://bioone.org/journals/bulletin-of-the-american-museum-of-natural-history/volume-2012/issue-372/803.1/Osteology-of-Khaan-mckennai-Oviraptorosauria-Theropoda/10.1206/803.1.pdf
  - The holotype `IGM/MPC-D 100/1127` and referred specimen
    `IGM/MPC-D 100/1002` are nearly complete and articulated.
  - The referred `IGM/MPC-D 100/973` preserves a disarticulated skull and
    parts of the pelvis and hind limbs.
  - The skull roof is rounded and ends in a nearly vertical occiput. The nasal
    is pneumatized but is not enlarged into a cranial crest.
  - The external naris is narrow, slitlike, and slopes posterodorsally.
  - The toothless beak is broad, and the triturating surface is U-shaped.

## Evidence boundary

- `Khaan` is a small, gracile, crestless oviraptorid. It must not inherit the
  low integrated crest and nearly rectangular skull of `Citipati`.
- The dorsal nasal has only a small low midline ridge. Do not turn that ridge
  into a visible casque, horn, or broad cranial crest.
- The rostral edge slopes posteroventrally rather than forming the near-
  vertical premaxillary profile used for `Citipati`.
- The hand has the theropod phalangeal formula 2-3-4. Digit II is longest,
  digit III is nearly as long, and the robust digit I is about half as long.
- The nearly complete tail includes about 28 caudal vertebrae. No direct
  `Khaan` pygostyle evidence was found in the consulted species sources.
- Body feathers, arm feathers, a modest terminal feather fan, skin, and all
  coloration are comparative oviraptorosaur reconstructions, not direct
  `Khaan` soft-tissue evidence.
- No nest or egg association is treated as species-level evidence for
  `Khaan`.

## Hard identity gates

- One short, deep, rounded skull with a nearly vertical rear profile and no
  tall crest, casque, horn, or parrot-like crown.
- One broad, toothless beak. Reject visible teeth, a long narrow bird bill,
  or a hooked modern-raptor beak.
- One narrow, posterodorsally slanted external naris. Reject a round pinhole
  nostril or a large circular opening.
- A short, gracile oviraptorid neck and compact torso. Reject a swan, ostrich,
  cassowary, turkey, or generic long-necked bird silhouette.
- Exactly two forelimbs. Each hand has three clawed digits when readable.
- Exactly two hind limbs and two separate feet in complete-body views.
- No enlarged dromaeosaur-like sickle claw.
- One continuous tail originating at the pelvis. Reject a second, forked,
  detached, or abruptly cropped tail.
- A tail fan may be modest and explicitly comparative. Reject a peacock fan,
  species-level pygostyle claim, or duplicated fan.
- Reject extra heads, arms, hands, legs, feet, fingers, tails, or duplicated
  joints.

## Habitat and behavior gates

- Use reddish cross-bedded dunes, lower interdune surfaces, temporary runoff,
  shallow rain washes, and sparse non-flowering vegetation as a restrained
  Ukhaa Tolgod reconstruction.
- Avoid modern grassland, flowering shrubs, palms, uniform conifer forest, or
  treating today's exposed outcrop as an exact living landscape.
- Heavy rain and dune collapse are taphonomic context. A post-rain wash is a
  reconstruction, not direct evidence that the pictured animal foraged there.
- `MPC-D 100/1127` and `MPC-D 100/1002` were found about 20 cm apart in the
  same bedding plane and are similar-sized adults. Their proximity does not
  establish a pair bond, courtship, sex, mating, or stable social behavior.
- A distant small hornless protoceratopsid may be used only as broad regional
  community context. Do not label it `Protoceratops andrewsi`, and do not
  imply pursuit, predation, or direct association.

## Consistent phenotype

- Body plumage: smoky warm gray.
- Forelimb feathers: charcoal.
- Shoulder mantle: muted copper-russet.
- Underside: warm ivory.
- Throat accent: deep berry crescent.
- Cheek accent: muted blue-green.
- Pattern: restrained cream bars only on distal arm feathers and the modest
  tail fan.
- Beak: matte gray; lower legs: dark scaled gray.
- Keep saturation low and natural. No neon magenta, cyan wash, repeated body
  rings, dense spots, or synthetic checker patterns.

## Six gallery roles

1. Full-body rounded-skull and slit-naris representative.
2. Reverse-facing dawn walk preserving the same phenotype and anatomy.
3. High-oblique post-rain interdune foraging hypothesis.
4. Intentional head-to-wrist anatomy crop showing the broad beak, slit naris,
   and exactly three digits on each hand; do not make foot or tail claims.
5. Two separated similar-sized adults as a cautious same-bedding-plane social
   context, with no pair-bond or sex claim.
6. One full-body `Khaan` with one distant hornless protoceratopsid as broad
   community context, with no contact or named-species claim.
