## Evidence boundary

- Current restrictive treatment represents `E. ranzii` securely with the
  holotype `MCSNB 2888`.
- The holotype preserves a complete skull and much of the articulated
  postcranium, but lacks the caudal region, pelvis, and most hind limbs.
- `MCSNB 8950`, formerly described as a juvenile `E. ranzii` with membrane
  traces, is now treated as a distinct unnamed pterosaur. It cannot establish
  species-level wing, hind-wing, body-covering, or tail-vane anatomy here.
- A long tail and the detailed hind-limb silhouette are phylogenetic
  reconstructions for an early non-pterodactyloid pterosaur. Keep them
  conservative and label them as inferred.
- No direct `E. ranzii` terminal tail-vane evidence was found. Do not add a
  diamond, leaf, fork, or paddle vane.
- Fine body filaments are a pterosaur-wide inference, not preserved on the
  holotype.

## Hard identity gates

- A small Late Triassic non-pterodactyloid pterosaur, not a dinosaur, lizard,
  crocodile, modern bird, bat, or dragon.
- One long, low, narrow, lightweight and crestless skull with a large orbit.
- Separate external naris and smaller triangular antorbital fenestra; do not
  merge them into a pterodactyloid nasoantorbital opening.
- No cranial crest, horn, keratinous beak, or deep monitor-lizard head.
- Strongly heterodont tooth row: a few large anterior conical fangs followed
  by many much smaller closely packed tri- to pentacuspid teeth. Reject a row
  of uniform crocodile or theropod teeth.
- Exactly two forelimbs, and each forelimb is a wing. No separate birdlike
  arms beneath the wings.
- Each wing is supported by one continuous elongated fourth finger.
- Three small free clawed digits at each wrist when readable; these are not
  extra arms.
- Exactly two reconstructed hind limbs and two separate feet.
- One continuous, moderately long, straight, tapering tail inferred from the
  early-pterosaur body plan.
- No terminal tail vane, tail paddle, duplicated tail, or membrane attached
  as a second tail.
- Fine short body filaments and thin continuous wing membranes; reject giant
  pebbly scales, bird flight feathers, or bat-like multiple long wing digits.
- Reject extra heads, legs, wings, hands, tails, membranes, or duplicated
  joints.

## Habitat and behavior gates

- Use a low carbonate-island margin, sheltered freshwater reservoir, and
  distant warm intraplatform sea as a cautious regional reconstruction.
- Vegetation may include irregular Triassic conifers, cycads, seed ferns, and
  horsetails. Avoid modern pine plantations, grass, flowers, and palms.
- Do not turn the anoxic fossil basin floor into a literal living surface.
- Fish may appear as a separated shoal beneath the animal because fish
  consumption has direct support. Capture method, skimming, diving, and exact
  prey taxon remain hypotheses.
- Ground stance, launch, rest, or low flight remain reconstruction
  hypotheses. Captions must say so.

## Consistent phenotype

- Body: deep claret-brown mixed with charcoal umber.
- Wing membranes: muted sea-glass green-grey.
- Accent: restrained oxidized copper across the crown, cheek, and upper neck.
- Underside: pale parchment cream.
- Pattern: broad asymmetric smoke marbling and broken limestone-toned edge
  fields, never regular dots, chevrons, tail rings, or neon stripes.

## Six gallery roles

1. Full-body representative in a readable neutral low glide.
2. Reverse-facing color and membrane-pattern view of the same phenotype.
3. High-oblique wide habitat view over a freshwater-bearing carbonate island
   with the intraplatform sea in the distance.
4. Intentional head-to-planted-wrist crop emphasizing the separate skull
   openings, differentiated tooth row, three free wrist digits, and the
   folded fourth-finger wing support.
5. Distant, non-contact fish-shoal interaction hypothesis with no capture.
6. Firm-limestone quadrupedal stance or folded-wing rest hypothesis with four
   ground contacts and one inferred tapering tail.
