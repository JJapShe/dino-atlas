# Sinovenator changii reference library

Retrieved: 2026-07-31

This folder separates direct fossil evidence, openly licensed comparison
material, and generated reconstruction hypotheses. Reference images guide
anatomy only; generated gallery art does not copy a published restoration.

## Direct evidence

### Xu et al. 2002

- Citation: Xu, X., Norell, M. A., Wang, X.-L., Makovicky, P. J. and Wu,
  X.-C. 2002. A basal troodontid from the Early Cretaceous of China.
  Nature 415, 780-784.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/415780a
- Author-hosted PDF used for review:
  https://www.ivpp.cas.cn/sourcedb/zw/rck/yszj/202311/P020140314363714601601.pdf
- Copyright: publisher article; reviewed locally but not redistributed in this
  project.
- Evidence used: body length below about one metre, shallow triangular skull,
  large naris, densely packed teeth, long forelimb, five sacrals, posteriorly
  directed pubis, short slender metatarsal II, subarctometatarsalian foot, and
  lowest-Yixian fluvial occurrence.
- Boundary: the Lujiatun facies did not preserve Sinovenator soft tissue.
  Filament coverage in the generated images is comparative inference, not
  direct integument evidence for this taxon.

### Yin, Pei and Zhou 2018

- Article: https://peerj.com/articles/4977/
- License: CC BY 4.0.
- Local files:
  - `yin-pei-zhou-2018-sinovenator-cranial-morphology-cc-by.pdf`
  - `sinovenator-pmol-ad00102-skull-figure2-cc-by.jpg`
- Evidence used: PMOL-AD00102 preserves an almost complete skull, partial
  mandibles, and six cervical vertebrae; the orbit is large and the specimen
  comes from the lowest Yixian Formation at Lujiatun.

### Yu et al. 2024

- Article: https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-05832-3
- PDF: https://www.nature.com/articles/s42003-024-05832-3.pdf
- License: CC BY 4.0.
- Local files:
  - `yu-et-al-2024-sinovenator-brain-and-skeleton-cc-by-4.pdf`
  - `sinovenator-ivpp-v20378-skull-skeleton-figure1-cc-by-4.png`
- Evidence used: IVPP V20378 is a nearly complete articulated skeleton in a
  curled resting posture, with relatively long forelimbs, elongated caudals,
  a shallow compressed rostrum, a large naris, dense teeth, and enlarged
  distal claws on pedal digit II.
- Boundary: the fossil posture supports a curled-resting reconstruction, but
  does not by itself prove sleep, behavior, sociality, color, or soft tissue.

## Explicit rejects

The three former project files remain archived but are excluded from current
review and gallery selection:

- `sinovenator-changii-yixian-troodontid-imagegen-v1.png`: broad modern-bird
  arm fans, exaggerated movie-raptor foot cues, and unreliable digit layout.
- `sinovenator-changii-slate-jade-pattern-imagegen-v1.png`: the same unstable
  anatomy plus regular tail markings that weaken phenotype continuity.
- `sinovenator-changii-yixian-lakemargin-ecology-imagegen-v1.png`: repeated
  foreground animals and under-audited appendages in an overly generic
  lake-margin setting.

Generation drafts with a fifth toe, three grounded forward toes plus a raised
digit II, or an unclear digit-II position were also rejected before project
copy. They remain outside `assets/dinosaurs/`.

## Reconstruction boundaries

- Short body filaments, colors, pattern, eye color, skin, and feather
  distribution are comparative hypotheses.
- The representative palette and the aubergine-celadon variant are plausible
  visual systems, not fossil color claims.
- Lujiatun is rendered as a fluvial volcaniclastic surface. Exact weather,
  vegetation density, water state, and moment are reconstruction choices.
- The foot close-up is a cautious living reconstruction of digits I-IV. It
  visualizes the fossil evidence but is not a preserved soft-tissue footprint.
- Only the first gallery image is a `count-level pass`. The color role remains
  `review hold`; habitat, skull, foot, and resting roles remain review/reference
  images and do not outrank the representative.
