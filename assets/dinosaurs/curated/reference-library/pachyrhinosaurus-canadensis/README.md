## Evidence boundary used in the gallery

- Direct or species-diagnostic skull evidence: a massive low nasal boss instead of a nasal horn, low supraorbital bosses close to the nasal boss, absence of the rostral comb seen in `P. lakustai` and `P. perotorum`, and the `P. canadensis` posterior frill ornament pattern.
- Genus-level comparative evidence: rough cornified boss covering, growth-related boss transformation, and large laterally curved P3 horns.
- Comparative life reconstruction: continuous skin over the bony frill openings, body proportions, feet, soft-tissue thickness, color, pattern, and exact horn sheath length.
- Habitat reconstruction: fluvial and deltaic sand, silt, coal-rich layers, shallow channels, conifers, low angiosperms, ferns, and horsetails. Exact plant placement, water level, weather, and seasonal timing are hypotheses.
- Behavior and association: the separated `Albertosaurus` and two-adult scenes show regional context or cautious proximity only. They do not establish attack, pursuit, herd structure, pair bonding, sex, courtship, parenting, dominance, or migration.

## Rejection gates

- Reject a round white ball or upright horn on the nasal boss, any long nasal or brow horn, a rostral comb, a median `P. lakustai`-style frill horn, forward `P. perotorum`-style frill horns, a radial `Styracosaurus` spike fan, or open/transparent frill holes.
- Reject rhinoceros, bison, elephant, or generic `Triceratops` body drift; mammal ears and hoof feet; extra or merged limbs, feet, tails, heads, eyes, nostrils, or horns.
- A visible side must have one eye in the normal postorbital position and one small matte nostril near the beak. A glossy oval on the snout that reads as a second eye is a hard failure.
