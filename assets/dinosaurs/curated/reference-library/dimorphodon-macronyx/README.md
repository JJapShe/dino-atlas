# Dimorphodon macronyx reference library

This folder is for anatomy review and generation QA. Use the sources to verify
proportions and diagnostic structures; do not copy an existing artwork's
composition, surface treatment, or style.

## Local public-domain source

- Richard Owen, historical specimen plate reproduced as `Macronyx.jpg`
- Wikimedia Commons:
  https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Macronyx.jpg
- Rights status: public domain
- Local original:
  `owen-history-british-fossil-reptiles-dimorphodon-skull-public-domain.jpg`

The plate is useful as a historical record of the skull, jaws, tooth row, and
associated disarticulated material. It does not provide a clean modern
whole-body reconstruction. Owen's interpretation is historical and must not
override later osteological work.

## Current and diagnostic sources kept as links

- Sangster, 2021/2022, current osteological review:
  https://doi.org/10.1080/02693445.2021.2037868
  - Use as the current specimen-level authority. It is linked rather than
    redistributed here.
- Ezcurra, 2016, emended diagnosis:
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4860341/
  - Strongly heterodont dentition: long, slender anterior teeth and small,
    closely packed lancet-shaped posterior teeth.
  - No skull-roof crest.
  - The external naris occupies most of the lateral snout.
  - No downturned dentary tip; an external mandibular fenestra is present.
- Padian, 1983, Yale material and functional morphology:
  https://elischolar.library.yale.edu/peabody_museum_natural_history_postilla/189/
  - Useful specimen history and skeletal evidence. Some locomotor
    interpretations are historical and should be checked against Witton 2015.
- Pugh et al., 2014, Blue Lias depositional setting:
  https://doi.org/10.1111/let.12075
  - The fossil-bearing Blue Lias records a marine shelf limestone and mudstone
    setting. It does not prove that the living animal occupied modern-looking
    grassy sea cliffs or tide pools.

## Hard identity gates

- An Early Jurassic non-pterodactyloid pterosaur, not a dinosaur, lizard,
  modern bird, bat, or dragon.
- One relatively large but lightweight fenestrated head; do not inflate it
  into a monitor-lizard or puffin-shaped head.
- Gently convex, short-to-moderate snout with a very large external naris.
- No cranial crest, horn, keratinous beak, or downturned jaw tip.
- Heterodont teeth: several long slender anterior teeth followed by much
  smaller, closely packed posterior teeth. Reject uniform theropod teeth.
- Exactly two forelimb wings. Each wing is supported by one continuous,
  elongated fourth finger.
- Three small free clawed digits at each wrist when readable; these are not
  extra arms.
- Exactly two robust hind limbs and two feet.
- One long, stiff, single tail tapering to a fine point.
- No large diamond, oval, forked, or leaf-shaped tail vane. A Dimorphodon
  terminal vane is not directly evidenced.
- Fine pycnofibre body covering and restrained skin texture; reject giant
  pebbly lizard scales or feathers.
- Reject extra heads, legs, wings, hands, tails, membranes, or duplicated
  joints.

## Habitat and behavior gates

- Prefer a firm coastal-lowland or inland-margin substrate with sparse Early
  Jurassic ferns, cycads, horsetails, and low conifers.
- Marine water may appear as distant regional context only.
- Avoid modern grass, flowering plants, dramatic modern chalk cliffs, and
  confident tide-pool specialization.
- Ground walking, cautious climbing, resting, or low flight are reconstruction
  hypotheses. Keep captions explicit about that status.
- Any small animal or invertebrate remains distant and unidentified. Do not
  show capture, killing, or a named prey relationship.

## Consistent phenotype

- Body: deep moss-charcoal with a muted bottle-green cast.
- Wing membranes: smoky blue-grey.
- Accent: irregular pale amber and stone marbling across the membranes, with a
  restrained rust-copper throat and cheek.
- Underside: warm grey-ivory.
- Pattern: asymmetric, low-contrast, and natural. No regular spots, neon
  stripes, or synthetic gradients.

## Six gallery roles

1. Full-body representative in a readable neutral low glide.
2. Reverse-facing color and wing-pattern view of the same phenotype.
3. High-oblique coastal-lowland habitat view after rain.
4. Intentional head-to-planted-wrist crop emphasizing the naris, heterodont
   teeth, three free wrist digits, and folded fourth-finger wing support; do
   not make a distal limb or tail count claim from this crop.
5. Distant, non-capture interaction hypothesis with one unidentified small
   animal.
6. Firm-ground locomotion hypothesis with folded wings and all four limbs
   readable.
