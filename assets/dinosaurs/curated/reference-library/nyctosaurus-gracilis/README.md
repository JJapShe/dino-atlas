# Nyctosaurus gracilis reference library

This folder is for anatomy review and generation QA. Use the sources to verify
skeletal proportions and diagnostic structures; do not copy an existing
artwork's composition, surface treatment, or style.

## Research sources kept as links

- Unwin, 2003, pterosaur phylogeny:
  https://repository.geologyscience.ru/bitstream/handle/123456789/35124/Unwi_03.pdf?sequence=1
  - Diagnosis used here: loss of free manual digits I-III, a wing finger
    reduced to three phalanges, a hatchet-shaped humeral deltopectoral crest,
    T-shaped mid-notarial neural spines, and crescentic dorsal centra.
- Bennett, 2003, branching-crested *Nyctosaurus* specimens:
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/BF03004560
  - The extraordinary branching crest is documented for two genus-level
    specimens and may be a late-ontogenetic display structure. It is not a
    secure fixed trait for a representative *N. gracilis* image.
- Hattin, 1982, Smoky Hill Chalk depositional environment:
  https://www.kgs.ku.edu/Publications/Bulletins/225/07_depo.html
  - The setting was the broad Western Interior seaway, with carbonate mud
    deposited far from major terrigenous sources. Avoid a modern grassy
    chalk-cliff foreground.
- Bestwick et al., 2018, review of pterosaur diet evidence:
  https://pure-oai.bham.ac.uk/ws/files/49117931/Bestwick_et_al_2018_Biological_Reviews.pdf
  - Piscivory is usable only as a cautious hypothesis. Do not depict a
    speculative capture or skimming behavior as established fact.

## Hard identity gates

- A nyctosaurid pterosaur, not a dinosaur, modern bird, or bat.
- Long, straight, toothless beak.
- Representative images are crestless.
- Exactly two forelimb wings with smooth, clawless wrist margins.
- No free manual digits or claws I-III.
- Exactly three long jointed wing-finger phalanges on each side.
- Exactly two separate small hind legs with two visible feet.
- One very short tail.
- Reject long or forked tails, extra limbs, extra membranes, teeth, wrist
  hands/claws, or bird/bat anatomy.

## Habitat and behavior gates

- Use open Western Interior Seaway water, distant low shore at most, and a
  chalk/marl carbonate-sea context.
- Do not use modern grass, dramatic chalk cliffs, or a contemporary seabird
  colony.
- Fish or surface scanning may appear only as a labeled feeding hypothesis.
- A lower-rank comparison image may pair a crestless *N. gracilis* phenotype
  with one mature, branching-crested genus-level *Nyctosaurus*. Keep the
  animals separated, do not identify the crested animal as securely
  *N. gracilis*, and do not add an unsupported soft-tissue sail between the
  crest branches.

## Consistent phenotype

- Body: deep marine charcoal-blue.
- Wing membranes: muted blue-green to celadon.
- Accent: restrained rust or ochre around the throat and eye.
- Beak and underside: warm ivory.
- Pattern: subtle natural countershading and low-contrast mottling; no neon,
  synthetic stripes, or regular dots.

## Six gallery roles

1. Crestless representative in full-body flight.
2. Color and pattern view of the same phenotype.
3. Open Western Interior Seaway habitat view.
4. Identity/anatomy view emphasizing clawless wrists and three wing segments.
5. Cautious fish-shoal or surface-scanning interaction hypothesis.
6. Lower-rank growth/display comparison with one genus-level crested specimen.
